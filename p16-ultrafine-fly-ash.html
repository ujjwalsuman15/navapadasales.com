Ultrafine fly ash is a very fine pozzolanic material produced from selected fly ash through classification. BIS describes ultrafine fly ash as consisting of very fine, glassy and generally spherical particles, with an average particle size around 3–5 microns.

What is Ultrafine Fly Ash?

Ultrafine Fly Ash is a supplementary cementitious material with much finer particles than conventional fly ash. The smaller particle size increases available reactive surface area and improves particle packing, helping create a denser cementitious matrix when used in a properly designed concrete mix.

Under IS 19058:2024, ultrafine fly ash is covered for use in concrete, mortar and other hydraulic-cement systems. The standard describes its production by multi-stage classification of fly ash conforming to IS 3812 (Part 1).

Why Ultrafine Fly Ash Matters in Concrete

Its fine particle size can improve packing and pozzolanic reactivity, supporting stronger, denser and more durable concrete systems. Actual performance depends on the concrete mix, dosage, raw-material quality and project requirements.

Ultrafine Fly Ash vs Conventional Fly Ash

The main distinction is fineness. Ultrafine fly ash has a much smaller particle size, which increases surface area and can accelerate pozzolanic contribution while improving particle packing.
