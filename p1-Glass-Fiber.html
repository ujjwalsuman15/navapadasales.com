What is Glass Fibre?

Glass Fibre is a high-strength reinforcement material manufactured from specially formulated molten glass that is drawn into extremely fine filaments. These fibres possess exceptional tensile strength, dimensional stability, and resistance to environmental exposure.

In construction applications, glass fibres are uniformly dispersed within concrete, mortar, or specialised cementitious mixes to improve mechanical performance and minimise crack formation.

For applications involving GFRC concrete in Surat, Alkali-Resistant (AR) Glass Fibre is commonly used because it is specifically designed to withstand the highly alkaline environment of cement without significant degradation over time.

MANUFACTURING PROCESS

Manufacturing Process of Glass Fibre

High-quality glass fibre production requires advanced manufacturing technology, strict quality control, and precision engineering.

01

Raw Material Selection

Carefully selected raw materials including silica sand, limestone, alumina, and other mineral additives contribute to the final mechanical properties.

02

Glass Melting

Raw materials are melted in high-temperature furnaces exceeding 1,400°C to form homogeneous molten glass.

03

Fibre Formation

Molten glass passes through precision platinum bushings with microscopic openings to produce continuous glass filaments.

04

Cooling

Newly formed fibres are rapidly cooled to preserve their strength and structural integrity.

05

Surface Treatment

Specialised sizing or protective coating improves handling, dispersion, cement bonding, and long-term durability.

06

Chopping Process

Continuous strands are cut into predetermined lengths suitable for concrete reinforcement.

07

Quality Testing

Batches are tested for fibre length, tensile strength, alkali resistance, moisture, dispersion, diameter uniformity, and coating quality.

08

Packaging

Finished fibres are packed in moisture-resistant packaging to maintain quality during storage and transportation.
